Was it Macedonia, or North Macedonia?

For 27 years, mapmakers had to pick a side in a naming dispute between two countries. Then, in 2019, the dispute actually got resolved — a rare outcome in this series.

Before 12 Feb 2019
Since 12 Feb 2019
Greece's objection

"Macedonia" is also a Greek region

Greece has its own historic region called Macedonia, home to Thessaloniki and tied to the legacy of Alexander the Great. Greece argued that a neighboring country using the name "Macedonia" alone implied a claim on that shared heritage — and, some feared, on Greek territory.

The country's position

A name chosen at independence

When the country declared independence from Yugoslavia in 1991, it adopted the name of the region it primarily occupied. It saw "Macedonia" as its own national and ethnic identity, not a territorial claim on Greece.

The border on the map never moved. The name attached to it did. Greece blocked the country's UN membership under its chosen name in 1993 (it was admitted under the placeholder "the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia") and later blocked its NATO accession in 2008 over the same dispute. The 2018 Prespa Agreement between the two governments settled it: the country would be called the Republic of North Macedonia, in exchange for Greece dropping its objections. The rename took effect on 12 February 2019, and the country joined NATO the following year.
Hanten Principle: Most disputed labels on a map don't get a tidy ending — this one did.
Compared with the rest of this series, North Macedonia is the exception that proves the rule: a name dispute is a political choice, not a fixed fact — which means it can also be un-chosen.
