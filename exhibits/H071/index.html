Does your birth month help decide whether you make the NHL?

Birthdays should be spread evenly through the year, about 25% in each quarter. Among NHL draftees, they aren't.

Kids born just after the age cutoff are almost a year older than their teammates, and coaches mistake that year for talent. Canadian youth hockey groups players by birth year, so a child born January 1 plays alongside one born December 31. At age eight, that extra year is about 12% more life: bigger, stronger, and better coordinated. Those kids make the top teams, get more coaching and ice time, and the head start grows. By draft day the age gap no longer matters, but the selection already happened.

Any system that compares people at one moment can mistake an early start for ability. The same pattern appears in school readiness, gifted programs, and youth soccer.

The twist at the top

The same study found that players born late in the year who did get drafted went on to play more NHL games, on average, than early-born draftees picked at the same spot. Because they had to overcome the disadvantage, the ones who made it tended to be better than their draft position suggested. Teams were, in effect, passing over talent.
