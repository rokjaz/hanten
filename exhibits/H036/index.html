What is the world’s tallest mountain called?

One mountain has three widely recognized names — and the most familiar English name is the newest.

Tibetan name, official in China

Chomolungma

The original exhibit gives this as the Tibetan name, “Goddess Mother of the World,” used for centuries before Western surveyors reached the region. It is used officially in China today (珠穆朗玛峰).

Nepal’s official name

Sagarmatha

The original exhibit translates this as “Forehead of the Sky” and says Nepal adopted it as the country’s official name in the 1960s for government and educational use.

The Western name

Everest

The original exhibit says the Royal Geographical Society adopted this name in 1865 at the suggestion of Surveyor General Andrew Waugh, honoring his predecessor Sir George Everest.

The mountain had names long before it was called Everest. Chomolungma and Sagarmatha reflect regional naming traditions; “Everest” entered Western cartography in the nineteenth century.
Explore how “Everest” became the Western name

The Royal Geographical Society adopted “Everest” in 1865 after Surveyor General Andrew Waugh proposed naming the peak for his predecessor, Sir George Everest. The original exhibit notes that George Everest himself reportedly objected and favored local names where they existed.
