California has more car thefts than anywhere else in the U.S. Is it the riskiest place to park?

The count is real. But a bigger place has more of almost everything, including car thefts. To compare risk, divide by how many people live there.

A total mostly tells you how big a place is. California has 56 times as many people as Washington, D.C., so it would have far more car thefts even if the risk were the same. Divide by the number of residents and the ranking flips: a car in D.C. was about twice as likely to be stolen.

The same trap shows up in any comparison of totals between groups of different sizes: crimes by city, accidents by car brand, complaints by airline, cases by county. Before comparing two totals, ask how big each group is, and look for the number per person.

Count only violent crime and Washington and Oregon look better than average. Count property crime, like car theft and burglary, and they're near the bottom. "Safest states" and "safest cities" lists each choose which crimes to count and how much weight to give each one, so the same place can land near the top of one list and near the bottom of another. Before trusting a ranking, check what it counted, and whether it divided by population at all.

Same trap, different product: which state buys the most Ford F-150s?

Wyoming, per person. Texas, in total. Wyoming has the highest share of F-150s among its vehicles, but Texas is so much bigger that it has far more F-150 owners. Neither answer is wrong; “most” changes when the measure changes.
