Is Reno, Nevada west of Los Angeles?

Reno reads as an "east of California" city. By longitude it isn't — draw a true north–south line through downtown Los Angeles, and Reno falls on the ocean side of it.

LA's line of longitude Reno, NV Los Angeles, CA

Why the coastline lies. California's coast isn't a vertical line — it leans. The northern border sits near 124.2°W; by San Diego the coast has drifted to about 117.2°W, nearly seven degrees east over roughly 800 miles of shoreline. The hinge is around Point Conception, near Santa Barbara: north of it, the coast runs roughly northwest–southeast as expected; south of it, the coast — and Los Angeles with it — swings sharply east. Reno sits in the less-drifted northern zone, just inside Nevada by Lake Tahoe, so it ends up west of a city that reads, on a mental map, as clearly "in California."

It isn't only Reno. San Francisco, on the undrifted northern coast, is a full 228 miles west of LA's meridian — no surprise there. But San Diego, another 120 miles down the coast from LA, is actually about 63 miles east of it. And Las Vegas, which most people mentally file next to Reno, sits roughly 174 miles east of LA's meridian — more than three degrees of longitude from its fellow Nevada city.
