After a goat is revealed, should you stay or switch?

Pick a door. The host will reveal a goat behind one of the other doors. Then make your choice.

The Monty Hall Problem
Run the numbers

A few rounds can feel like luck. Ten thousand rounds reveal the pattern.

Switching wins about twice as often.

Your first pick starts with a 1-in-3 chance. The host's informed reveal does not reset the two remaining doors to 50–50.

Why does switching work?

Your original choice is correct only 1 time out of 3. The other two doors collectively begin with a 2-in-3 chance. Because the host knows where the car is and always opens a goat door you did not choose, that 2-in-3 chance is concentrated on the one other unopened door. If your original pick was wrong — which happens 2 times out of 3 — switching wins.

Source note — Steve Selvin posed the problem in The American Statistician in 1975; it became widely known after Marilyn vos Savant discussed it in Parade in 1990.
