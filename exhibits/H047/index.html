Is it true that more people are alive today than have ever died?

You've probably heard this claim. Population growth has been fast enough to make it sound plausible. It isn't close to true.

The math looks close until you count all of human history. Global population has more than tripled since 1950, and that growth is real. But about 117 billion people have ever been born, so the 8.2 billion alive today are only about 7% of all humans who have ever existed.

The same illusion shows up anywhere a fast recent trend is compared with a much longer, quieter history underneath it. The recent part is the part you lived through and can feel, so the visible slice feels like the whole pie.

How anyone estimates births going back 192,000 years

There's no census for most of human history, so demographers reconstruct total births from what is known: rough population sizes at different points in the past, combined with typical birth rates for a population of that size and era. Early human populations were small, but birth rates (and death rates) were high, so a very long stretch of prehistory contributes a large share of the historical total. The Population Reference Bureau, which produced the widely cited 117-billion estimate, describes the exercise as "part science and part art": a defensible order-of-magnitude figure, not a precise count.

It is the same failure of intuition that How far apart are a million and a billion? points at directly: intuition is built for small, everyday quantities, and it doesn't scale up to hundreds of billions, or across two hundred thousand years.
