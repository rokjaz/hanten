Why does moving the axis change the story?
The rule: a bar's length is what our eyes read as its value, so a bar chart makes an implicit promise that its axis starts at zero. Break that promise — start the axis at 48 instead of 0 — and a 1.8-point gap gets stretched until one bar looks more than twice as tall as the other, even though neither number moved. This is one of the most common chart manipulations in the wild — cable news graphics, earnings decks, product comparisons — and the tell is the same every time: check where the axis starts before you read the bars.

The same instinct — pick the framing that tells the story you want — shows up whenever a single summary number stands in for a messier reality, the way one enormous income can drag an “average” income far away from what any typical person actually earns.
