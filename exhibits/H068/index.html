Which country has the most time zones?

Most people guess Russia or the United States. The answer is France, with 12, and only one of them is in France itself.

France has the most time zones because it's scattered, not because it's big. Mainland France runs on a single zone. The other 11 come from overseas departments and territories spread across every ocean, while Russia, by far the largest country, has 11.

A count of time zones measures how spread out a country is, and what its government chose. China is wide enough for about five zones and officially uses one. Rankings built on counts can reward being scattered as easily as being big.

When a ranking says “most,” is it measuring size or spread?

Scattered, not big

Mainland France runs on a single time zone. The other 11 come from overseas departments and territories left over from its colonial empire, including islands in the Pacific, the Caribbean, the North Atlantic, and the Indian Ocean, plus French Guiana on the South American mainland. France comes out on top because its land is spread across every ocean, not because it's big.

Size is a poor predictor. Russia is by far the world's largest country, and its 11 time zones, from Kaliningrad (UTC+2) to Kamchatka (UTC+12), are the most for any country on a single landmass. The United States also reaches 11, but only by counting territories out to American Samoa, Guam, and uninhabited Baker and Howland Islands (UTC−12). China shows how political the count really is: it's wide enough to span about five time zones, yet the whole country officially runs on one, UTC+8.

The answer also depends on what you count. France's figure is 12 for inhabited and administered territory, or 13 if you include Adélie Land, its claim in Antarctica. And a country "has" a time zone here only if some part of it keeps that offset, which is a political choice, not a geographic fact.

What about the opposite: a huge country on one clock?

China spans about four hours of sun time and uses one time zone. It is about as wide as the contiguous United States, which uses four, but since 1949 the whole country has run on Beijing time. In Kashgar, in the far west, that puts the winter sunrise after 10 a.m. A country’s clocks can follow the sun or follow the capital; that’s a political choice, not a fact of geography.
