55–27 Yankees in runs. 4–3 Pirates in games. New York concentrated its runs in three blowouts — 16–3, 10–0, 12–0. Pittsburgh distributed enough runs to win four separate games, sealed by Bill Mazeroski's walk-off home run in Game 7. The Pirates' −28 run differential is still the largest ever posted by a World Series winner.

It is the same mechanism our Gerrymandering exhibit shows with voters and districts: winning the units that count — games, or districts — can matter far more than winning the underlying total, whether that total is runs scored or votes cast.

See why total runs do not decide a series

Each World Series game is a separate contest. Winning 16–3 counts as one win, just like winning 3–2. Once a team has scored enough to win a game, additional runs increase the series run total but do not create additional wins.

It gets stranger: Yankees second baseman Bobby Richardson was named Series MVP — the only time a player from the losing team has won the award. He batted .367 and drove in a record 12 runs, mostly in the blowout wins that inflated New York's run total without deciding the series.
