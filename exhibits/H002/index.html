How far can you get from everyone?

At one spot in the South Pacific, the nearest land is more than 1,600 miles away.

At Point Nemo, the closest people can sometimes be in orbit. The nearest land is more than six times farther away than the ISS is above Earth.

The same trick works with any big number: a distance, a file size, a population count can all feel abstract until you compare it to something familiar. It's the comparison, not the number itself, that tells you whether something is actually close or far, big or small.

What seems far away only because you’ve never compared it to anything?

How are these distances being compared?

The 1,670-mile (2,688 km) distance is measured across Earth’s surface from Point Nemo to the nearest land. The ISS figure is its approximate altitude above Earth, about 254 miles (408 km) in this comparison. They are different kinds of distance, but the rings are drawn to the same scale to make the size contrast visible.

The same fix works for almost any huge number: find a comparison that is actually familiar. The distance between Earth and the Moon sounds enormous until you check how much of it is empty space versus how much is just the two bodies themselves.

Is the space station ever closer than the nearest country?

Yes, in 13 places. When the ISS passes over New Zealand, it is about 254 miles (408 km) overhead, roughly 129 miles (208 km) closer than the nearest point of Australia, 383 miles away. Twelve other places, mostly remote islands, share the distinction: their nearest neighboring country is farther away than the space station.
