Whose country is Crimea on the map?

The same peninsula can be drawn three different ways depending on what the boundary is meant to represent.

As shown in Russia
As shown in Ukraine
Common international treatment
Russian-map treatment

Fully integrated

The original exhibit says Russian government maps show Crimea with the same ordinary treatment as Russian territory, without a special disputed boundary.

Ukrainian-map treatment

Part of Ukraine

The original exhibit says Ukrainian maps do not draw a national boundary separating Crimea from the rest of Ukraine, reflecting Ukraine’s claim to the peninsula.

International-map treatment

Dashed or disputed

The original exhibit says many international maps use a dashed or otherwise distinct boundary to show the gap between territorial recognition and on-the-ground administration.

The coastline does not change. The meaning of the boundary does. Crimea is internationally recognized as part of Ukraine, while Russia has controlled and administered the peninsula since 2014. A map must decide whether its line is showing legal recognition, a territorial claim, or control on the ground.
