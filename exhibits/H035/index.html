What is Northern Ireland’s second-largest city called?

One city. Two names that carry different histories and contexts.

The legal, chartered name

Londonderry

The original exhibit says the name was granted by royal charter in 1613, when King James I awarded the walled city to London trading guilds that funded its construction. It remains the city’s official legal name.

The older, more commonly used name

Derry

The original exhibit traces Derry to the Irish “Doire,” meaning “oak grove,” and notes that the name predates the 1613 charter by centuries. It also says the local council itself uses Derry in its legal name.

The two names coexist because they answer different kinds of questions. The original exhibit notes that the local authority is legally named Derry City and Strabane District Council, while the city it governs remains officially Londonderry by charter. Attempts to make one name completely replace the other have not resolved the split.
Hanten Principle: A place can have one location and more than one legitimate naming context.
Geography can stay fixed while history, law, language, identity, and everyday usage preserve different labels.
