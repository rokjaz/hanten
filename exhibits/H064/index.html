What's the biggest country?

"Biggest" seems like the one geography superlative that shouldn't need a definition. But depending on whether you count water, and how much of the land is actually farmable, the ranking changes twice — and Canada, the textbook #2, ends up nowhere near the top.

Total area (including inland water) Land area (water excluded) Arable land

Canada is the world's 2nd-biggest country by total area, and only the 6th-biggest by farmable land. About 9% of its area is fresh water, which drops it to 4th by land alone, and only about 4.3% of its land is arable. The United States, with over 16% of its land arable, has the most farmland of any country, roughly tied with India.

Every ranking of "biggest" answers a narrower question than it seems to. Before accepting one, ask what's being counted: total area, dry land, or land people can actually use.

Three rankings, three different runners-up

Canada is the world's 2nd-biggest country by total area — and only the 6th-biggest by farmable land. Every bar above is drawn to the same scale, so its length is the country's real total area. The tick mark shows how much is dry land once inland water is subtracted. The teal bar shows how much is actually arable — land that could grow crops, the roughest available proxy for "usable." Notice how small the teal bars are next to the full territories.

Canada's slide isn't a data quirk — it's two real geographic facts compounding. About 9% of Canada's total area is fresh water (it holds more lakes than the rest of the world's countries combined), which alone drops it from 2nd to 4th once you count land only. Most of what's left is boreal forest, tundra, or Arctic permafrost — only about 4.3% of Canada's land is arable, one of the lowest shares of any large country. The United States, by contrast, has plenty of temperate, non-mountainous land suited to agriculture: over 16% of its land is arable, more than triple Canada's share — enough to make the 3rd-largest country by total area the largest by usable farmland, roughly tied with India, which is not among the six largest countries by area but has about half of its land in crops.
