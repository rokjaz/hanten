Whose territory is Arunachal Pradesh on the map?

The same territory can appear three different ways depending on what the map means by its boundary.

Indian-map treatment

An ordinary state

Indian maps show Arunachal Pradesh as an Indian state, using an ordinary internal boundary rather than a symbol suggesting that its status is disputed.

Chinese-map treatment

“South Tibet”

Chinese government maps show the same area as Chinese territory, commonly referring to it as Zangnan, or South Tibet.

International-map treatment

Dashed, disputed

Many international maps show the area under Indian administration while using a distinct boundary with China to indicate that the border is disputed.

The land stays where it is. The political meaning of the line changes. India administers Arunachal Pradesh; China claims most of the state. The disputed boundary follows the McMahon Line, which China does not accept.
