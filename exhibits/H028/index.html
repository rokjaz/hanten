Greenland looks almost as large as the Lower 48 on many world maps. How many Greenlands actually fit inside?

Put both at the same equal-area scale and the familiar picture changes fast.

Lower 48 ≈ 8.08 million km² · Greenland ≈ 2.17 million km² · ≈ 3.7 : 1
The contiguous United States is about 3.7 times larger than Greenland. On a Mercator map, Greenland is stretched dramatically because it sits so far north. Draw both with an equal-area projection at the same scale and the illusion disappears.
Each numbered shape is Greenland. The copies may be rotated because this comparison is about area, not orientation. The fourth copy deliberately spills beyond the U.S. outline: four full Greenlands would be too much, while three would leave a substantial amount of the country uncovered.

Hanten Principle: Human intuition about scale is unreliable.
A familiar map can preserve useful relationships while badly distorting area. Changing the projection can change what feels large.
