    Even mountains barely move the number.
    Alaska gains roughly 11,000 square miles when its terrain is unrolled — yet that is still less than 2% of its land area.

    Water matters more

    Michigan and Hawaii are each about 41% water by total state area. Michigan alone contains about 40,175 square miles of water — more than Kentucky's entire land area.

    Terrain is surprisingly small

    Washington, Idaho, and California are among the states where terrain adds the largest percentage, but all remain under about 1.5%. For most states, the gain is only a few hundredths of a percent.

    Source basis from the original H029 exhibit: total, land, and water area from the U.S. Census Bureau as reproduced in the source exhibit's state-area dataset. Terrain surface area was computed there from the GEBCO_2025 global elevation grid at 15 arc-second resolution (about 460 m cells), estimating each cell's true area as flat area × √(1 + slope²), then summing by state. Because the grid cannot capture every small-scale terrain irregularity, the original exhibit treats these surface ratios as a floor rather than a ceiling.
