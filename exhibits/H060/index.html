The NFL fixed the coin-toss advantage in overtime. Did it stay fixed?

In 2010, the NFL changed its overtime format specifically because winning the coin toss was too big an advantage. The fix worked for a few years — then a separate, unrelated rule change quietly undid it.

A perfectly fair coin toss would win the game exactly 50% of the time, on average, no matter who calls it.

Shortening the clock brought the imbalance back

Under sudden death (2005–2011), whoever scored first won, and a coin-toss-winning offense often only needed a field goal. The league fixed this in 2010–2012: the second team now gets a possession unless the first team scores a touchdown. It worked — the coin-toss winner's win rate dropped to a near-even 51.2% from 2012–2016.

Then, in 2017, the league shortened overtime from 15 minutes to 10 — a change aimed at player safety, unrelated to fairness. With less time on the clock, the second team gets fewer chances to answer a scoring drive. From 2017–2024, the coin-toss winner's win rate climbed to 59.7% — worse than the original sudden-death era the 2010 rule was designed to fix. In the playoffs under the post-2010 rule, coin-toss winners went 10–1 before the NFL made a further postseason-only tweak in 2022.

By 2024, winning the coin toss was a bigger advantage than it was before the league ever tried to fix it.

A system has many knobs — does fixing one really lock the others in place?

Sources: win-loss-tie records by era — 2005–2011 sudden death, 53-46-1 (53.5%); 2012–2016 modified rule at 15-minute overtime, 40-38-5 (51.2%); 2017–2024 modified rule at 10-minute overtime, 67-44-7 (59.7%); post-2010-rule playoff games, coin-toss winners 10-1. Figures per Sports Illustrated ("There Have Been 11 NFL Overtime Playoff Games Since the Rule Change," Jan. 2022) and subsequent league-data reporting through the 2024 season. The NFL made a further postseason-only overtime change in 2022, guaranteeing both teams a possession, after the 2022 AFC Championship Game ended without the trailing team getting the ball.
