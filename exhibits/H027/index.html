Did the United States grow one state at a time?

Put the order of admission onto the map — then look at the timing.

Earlier Later
1912 Arizona becomes the 48th state
47 YEARS →
1959 Alaska, then Hawaii

The sequence looks orderly. The timing was not. The original 13 entered in under three years — Delaware first, on December 7, 1787; Rhode Island last of the 13, on May 29, 1790. It then took another 169 years to add the remaining 37. After Arizona became No. 48 in February 1912, the country waited 47 years for Alaska, admitted January 3, 1959 — then only seven and a half months for Hawaii, on August 21, 1959.

A numbered list makes growth look steady by hiding exactly this: the gaps between the numbers can be wildly uneven even when the sequence itself is perfectly orderly.

Two Dakotas, one day

North Dakota and South Dakota were both admitted on November 2, 1889 — and, as the story goes, deliberately so: President Benjamin Harrison reportedly had the papers shuffled before signing so no one could ever say which territory became a state first. By convention the two are ordered alphabetically, North Dakota 39th and South Dakota 40th.

West Virginia becomes No. 35

Its territory had been part of Virginia — one of the original 13 — since 1788. When Virginia seceded to join the Confederacy in 1861, its Unionist mountain counties refused to go along, set up a rival government, and supplied their own consent to split off. Lincoln signed West Virginia into the Union on June 20, 1863.
