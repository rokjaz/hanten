How far apart are a million and a billion?

They sound one letter apart. But a billion isn't a bigger million; it's a thousand millions. A million seconds is a week and a half. A billion seconds is more than 30 years.

Million, billion, trillion: each word is 1,000 times the one before, but they sound like small steps. Our sense of size works fine for things we can count or see, and then quietly gives up. Past a certain point, "a lot" and "a thousand times more than a lot" feel about the same.

When a big number shows up in the news, a budget, or a population, translate it into something you can picture. Seconds work well: a million is days, a billion is decades, a trillion is all of recorded history several times over.

Where the numbers come from. A day has 86,400 seconds, so a million seconds is 11.57 days and a billion is 11,574 days, or 31.7 years. A U.S. bill is about 0.0043 inches thick, so 10,000 $100 bills ($1 million) stack about 43 inches high. The last Ice Age was at its coldest about 26,000 to 20,000 years ago, well within 31,700 years.
