How tall is the tallest mountain?

It depends which "tallest" you mean. Above sea level, base to summit, distance from the planet's center, or how much a peak stands on its own — each definition crowns a different champion.

Above sea level

8,849 m
Mount Everest
The measurement everyone means by default — highest point above mean sea level. Nepal/China border.

Base to summit

~10,210 m
Mauna Kea
Measured from its true base on the Pacific Ocean floor. Only the top 4,207 m shows above water.

Farthest from Earth's center

+2,072 m
Chimborazo
Sits near the equator, where the planet bulges outward — its summit reaches ~2,072 m farther into space than Everest's, despite being barely 6,263 m above sea level.

A fourth answer: how much a mountain stands on its own

There's a fourth honest measurement, and it flips the leaderboard completely: topographic prominence — roughly, how far you'd have to descend from a peak before you could climb to something taller. A mountain sitting alone on a plain scores high even if it's not very elevated; a mountain that's merely one bump on a long ridge of taller neighbors scores low, no matter how high above sea level it sits.

Rank the same five mountains by height above sea level, then by prominence, and three of them trade places dramatically — including the world's second-highest mountain, K2, which quietly falls out of the picture.

"Tallest mountain" isn't one fact — it's a choice of ruler, and every ruler crowns someone different.
