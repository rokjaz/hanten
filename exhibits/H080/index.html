All 50 states, re-ranked by the people living outside their biggest metro area

Red lines fall 7 or more places. Teal lines rise 5 or more.

A state's population is really two numbers added together: its biggest metro area and everyone else. South Carolina, Wisconsin, and Tennessee have people spread across several cities and many small towns, so they climb when the biggest metro is set aside. New Jersey, Arizona, Illinois, and Nevada are mostly one metro area with a much smaller state around it.

A big total can hide where it comes from. Before comparing two totals, ask whether they're built the same way.

How we counted. "Metro area" means the Census Bureau's metropolitan statistical area: a big city plus the suburbs around it. For each state, the biggest metro is the one with the most residents inside that state, and only that in-state part is subtracted. So New Jersey's is the New York City metro's New Jersey counties, Virginia's is the Washington, D.C., suburbs in Virginia, Delaware's is the Wilmington area of the Philadelphia metro, and New Hampshire's is the Boston metro's two New Hampshire counties. All five of Rhode Island's counties are in metro Providence, so nothing is left. The second ranking compares each state's "everyone else" with every other state's "everyone else."
