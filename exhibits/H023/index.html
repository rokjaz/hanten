Africa ≈ 11.7 million sq mi (30.3 million km²) · Greenland ≈ 836,000 sq mi (2.17 million km²)

A Mercator map draws Greenland slightly bigger than Africa. In reality, Africa is 14 times bigger. Mercator keeps compass directions true for navigation by stretching land more the farther it sits from the equator. Africa straddles the equator and barely stretches; Greenland, mostly above 60° N, is stretched more than any other large landmass.

Every flat map has to distort something: size, shape, distance, or direction. Before you compare sizes on a map, ask what that map was built to keep true.

Which is actually bigger, and what would you check before trusting the map?

Why does Mercator stretch Greenland so much?

Mercator widens every line of latitude to the same length as the equator, then stretches it north–south by the same amount to keep shapes and directions right. The result grows areas by a factor that climbs quickly toward the poles: about 1.3 times at 30°, 4 times at 60°, 10 times at 72° N (the middle of Greenland), and more than 30 times at Greenland’s northern tip.

Most of Africa lies between 35° N and 35° S, where the stretch stays under 1.5 times. That difference in stretching is what turns a 14-to-1 size gap into a map where Greenland looks bigger.

Greenland looks bigger than the Lower 48 too. Is it?

No. On a Mercator map, Greenland covers about two and a half times the map area of the contiguous United States. In reality, the Lower 48 is about 3,120,000 sq mi (8.08 million km²), big enough to hold about 3.7 Greenlands.

How big is Greenland compared with Africa’s largest countries?

Greenland lands almost exactly in the middle. On a Mercator map, Greenland covers about 12 times Algeria’s map area. At true size, Algeria and DR Congo are each larger than Greenland, and Sudan and Libya are only modestly smaller.
