Greenland looks almost as large as Africa on many world maps. How many Greenlands actually fit inside Africa?

Put Greenland and Africa on the same equal-area scale.

1The map you know. On a Mercator world map, Greenland looks nearly as big as Africa.
2At true size. Same scale, equal-area map: it takes 14 Greenlands to cover Africa.
Africa ≈ 11.7 million sq mi · Greenland ≈ 836,000 sq mi · Ratio ≈ 14:1

Africa is about 14 times larger than Greenland. Sitting mostly above 60°N, Greenland is the Mercator projection's biggest beneficiary — inflated until it looks like a rival to Africa. The 14 shapes above cover about 11.7 million sq mi (30.3 million km²), almost exactly matching Africa's true area: the first seven drop in cleanly, then the rest squeeze into whatever coastline is left, since real coastlines can't tile perfectly.

The same illusion flatters Greenland against the U.S. On a Mercator map, Greenland looks about two and a half times the size of the Lower 48. In reality, the Lower 48 is about 3,120,000 sq mi (8.08 million km²), big enough to hold about 3.7 Greenlands.

See how the comparison was made

Each numbered shape is Greenland rendered with its own azimuthal equal-area projection, scaled to match Africa's. The copies are rotated and repositioned only to make them fit visually — rotation does not change area.

Positions come from a packing search that minimizes overlap and ocean spill. Because real coastlines don't tile perfectly, the later pieces increasingly straddle the coast rather than sitting cleanly on land.

How big is Greenland compared with Africa’s largest countries?

Greenland lands almost exactly in the middle. On a Mercator map, Greenland covers about 12 times Algeria’s map area. At true size, Algeria and DR Congo are each larger than Greenland, by about 10% and 8%, while Sudan and Libya are only modestly smaller, by about 14% and 19%.
