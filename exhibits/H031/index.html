Does “…USA” on the label mean an American company made it here?

“Made in USA” and “Assembled in USA” sound almost the same, but they follow different rules. And neither one says anything about who owns the company. Most of the cars ranked most American-made are sold by companies based overseas.

Where it's made and who makes it are different questions. Cars.com ranks vehicles each year by where they're assembled and where their parts, engines, and transmissions come from. In 2026, two Teslas topped the list, but the other eight of the top 10 came from Honda, Toyota, and Stellantis, the Netherlands-based company that owns Jeep. Foreign automakers made up about 65% of the 86 vehicles that qualified. Meanwhile, some cars from American brands, like the Ford Maverick pickup, are built in Mexico.

Product labels are similar. “Made in USA” has a strict legal meaning; “Assembled in USA” has a looser one; and a company's nationality is a third question that neither label answers. Before deciding what a label tells you, ask which question it's actually answering.

What the rules actually say

“Made in USA” must mean that “all or virtually all” of the product is made in the United States: final assembly and all significant processing happen here, and all or virtually all of its parts and ingredients are made here too, with no more than negligible foreign content. The Federal Trade Commission enforces it.

“Assembled in USA” only requires that the product's principal assembly, a substantial one, took place in the U.S. The FTC's own example is a lawn mower with 15 to 20% foreign parts. Simply bolting imported parts together at the end doesn't qualify. Companies can also make qualified claims, such as “Made in USA of U.S. and imported parts” or “60% U.S. content.”

How Cars.com decides what's “American-made”

The American-Made Index scores each vehicle on five things: where it's assembled, how much of its parts content is from the U.S. and Canada under the American Automobile Labeling Act, how many U.S. factory workers the automaker employs relative to its production, and where the engine and transmission come from. The brand's home country isn't one of them. The 2026 list included 86 vehicles, down from 99 in 2025.
