Mosquitoes kill hundreds of thousands of people a year. Sharks kill a handful. Which is more dangerous?

Both charts below use real numbers, and they rank the animals in opposite order. "Most dangerous" can mean two different questions: how many people it kills in total, or how likely one encounter is to kill you.

"Most dangerous" is two different questions wearing the same words. Mosquitoes kill by far the most people because they bite billions of times a year, even though any single bite is very unlikely to kill. Sharks almost never meet people, but when one bites, it's far more likely to be fatal. Both rankings are true; they answer different questions.

The same switch happens with cars and planes, common and rare diseases, or big and small risks at work: total harm and risk per encounter can point in opposite directions. Before trusting a "most dangerous" ranking, ask whether it counts total deaths or the odds for one person.
