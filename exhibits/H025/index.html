If nothing bad happened, was the danger real?

When prevention works, the disaster never shows up, so the danger starts to look made up. Then people stop preventing it, and the danger comes back.

When prevention works, it erases its own evidence. Before the measles vaccine, 3 to 4 million Americans caught measles every year and 400 to 500 died. By 2000 the disease was eliminated in the U.S., and for many families it became something from history books. As vaccination slipped in some communities, measles returned: 285 cases in 2024, 2,289 in 2025 with three deaths, and 3,887 in 2026 by October 1. Early in 2025, 96% of cases were in people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccination status was unknown.

The same thing happens with levees, fire codes, and computer security: the better they work, the less there is to see, and the easier they are to cut. “Nothing happened” can mean the danger was never real, or that the prevention worked. Before deciding which, ask what would have happened without it.

Was Y2K real, or overhyped?

People still disagree, which is the paradox in action. Computers that stored years as two digits could read 2000 as 1900. Governments and companies spent an estimated $308 billion worldwide, about $134 billion of it in the United States, fixing systems before the date changed. Some failures still slipped through: computers handling data from U.S. spy satellites crashed for about two days, and in Sheffield, England, 154 pregnant women were given wrong risk results for Down syndrome.

Skeptics point out that some countries, such as South Korea, Italy, and Russia, spent much less and also saw few problems. Supporters answer that the quiet New Year is exactly what successful repairs look like. Once prevention has worked, there's no clean way to prove what would have happened without it.

Other places the paradox shows up

A levee that holds for decades starts to look like wasted money. A town that hasn't had a big fire in years questions its fire inspections. A company that hasn't been hacked trims its security budget. In each case, success is invisible: when prevention works, the result is that nothing happens.
