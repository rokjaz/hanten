Is Canada much bigger than Brazil?

On the Mercator map found on many classroom walls, Canada looks about 5½ times the size of Brazil. In reality it's only 17% bigger. And Alaska, which looks 3½ times the size of Mexico, is actually smaller.

Canada looks 5½ times Brazil’s size; it’s only 17% bigger. A Mercator map stretches places more the farther they are from the equator. Near the equator, Brazil barely changes. At 60° north, where much of Canada and Alaska sit, area is blown up about 4 times, and it keeps growing toward the pole. The map isn't wrong: Gerardus Mercator designed it in 1569 so sailors could draw a straight line and follow one compass heading. Keeping every coastline's shape true for navigation forces the far north and south to swell.

A tool built for one job can mislead you when it's used for another. The map that's perfect for steering a ship is a poor one for comparing how big countries are.

What do you judge by how big it looks on a map?

Why the stretch grows toward the poles

On a globe, lines of longitude squeeze together toward the poles. On a Mercator map they are drawn straight up and down, evenly spaced, so everything far from the equator is stretched sideways. At 60° north, the lines are really half as far apart as at the equator, so the map stretches that area 2 times sideways.

To keep shapes true, Mercator stretches north–south by the same amount. Two times sideways and two times up-and-down makes the area 4 times too big at 60°. At 70° it's about 8.5 times, and at 80° it's over 30 times, which is why Mercator maps usually stop well short of the poles.

Most of Canada and Alaska lies north of 50°, while Brazil and Mexico lie within about 35° of the equator. Measured across their real outlines, Canada is drawn about 5.1 times too large and Alaska about 5.4 times, while Brazil is only about 1.06 times and Mexico about 1.2 times.

Is Canada even the 2nd-biggest country? It depends what you count.

By total area, Canada is 2nd, behind only Russia. But about 9% of Canada is lakes and rivers: 344,000 sq mi (891,000 km²), more than the area of Texas. Count land only, and both China and the United States pass it.
