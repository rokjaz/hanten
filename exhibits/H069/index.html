How many of the world's people live north of the equator?

The equator divides Earth's surface into two equal halves. It is easy to picture people split roughly evenly too.

Share of each total north and south of the equator

The equator splits the globe evenly, but it doesn't split the people. About 87% of humanity lives north of the equator. The north holds about two-thirds of the land, including almost all of Asia, Europe, and North America, while much of the south is ocean. So a "typical" person on Earth has winter in January, sees the Big Dipper, and lives north of the line.

An even split of space rarely means an even split of what fills it. Before assuming a boundary divides things in half, check what is actually on each side.

Who lives in the south

The biggest populations south of the equator are in Brazil, the island of Java in Indonesia, South Africa, Tanzania, Argentina, and Australia. Even so, the whole Southern Hemisphere holds roughly as many people as India alone. Hemisphere shares vary a little by source and year; most estimates put the north between about 87% and 90%.
