How long would it take to drive to the International Space Station?

Photos make it look like deep space. It's actually only 254 miles (408 km) up — closer than some countries are to their own nearest neighbor.

Drive straight up at 65 mph 3 hr 54 min

The ISS orbits about 254 miles (408 km) above Earth.

Shrink Earth to a basketball 0.3 in

That's how far above the ball's surface the ISS would orbit.

Reaching the ISS’s height is a four-hour drive. Staying there takes 17,500 mph. At highway speed, the vertical trip takes less than four hours; the hard part of spaceflight is going sideways fast enough to keep missing Earth. Shrink Earth to a basketball and low Earth orbit is only a pencil-width away.

The Kármán line — the internationally recognized edge of space, 62 miles (100 km) up — is itself less than a quarter of the way to the ISS. Space starts a lot closer than orbit does.

Big numbers get easier to judge once you set them against a familiar scale, like a drive or a basketball. When a distance feels unimaginable, translate it into something you've actually experienced.

See the assumptions behind the comparison

The driving analogy assumes a constant 65 mph on an imaginary road straight upward. It ignores atmosphere, gravity, orbital mechanics, traffic, and the fact that the ISS is moving. The basketball comparison scales Earth's 7,918-mile (12,742 km) mean diameter to an official-size basketball about 9.4 inches across.
