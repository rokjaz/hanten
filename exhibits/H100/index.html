Ground beef labeled "80% lean." Is it a low-fat food?

The label is accurate: by weight, 80/20 ground beef is 80% lean and 20% fat. But weight isn't the only way to measure it. Count calories instead, and fat is most of what you're eating.

A true number can come in the unit that sounds best. "80% lean" measures weight, and most of that weight is water, which has no calories. Fat has more than twice the calories per gram of protein, so the 20% of fat by weight supplies about 71% of the calories.

The same choice shows up on plenty of labels: 2% milk is 2% fat by weight but gets about a third of its calories from fat, and a snack can be "95% fat-free" by weight while much of its energy comes from fat. When a label picks one unit, ask what the same fact looks like in another, equally true one.

Share of calories from fat is the fat grams × 9 calories, divided by total calories. U.S. labeling rules say that when a package states a lean percentage, it must show the fat percentage right next to it, in the same size and style of lettering.
