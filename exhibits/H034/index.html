What are these islands called?

One tiny island group appears under three different names.

Japan · current administrator

Senkaku Islands

Japan administers the islands as part of Ishigaki City, Okinawa Prefecture, and refers to them as the Senkaku Islands.

China · claimant

Diaoyu Islands

China claims the islands, cites historical records supporting its position, and refers to them as the Diaoyu Islands.

Taiwan · claimant

Diaoyutai Islands

Taiwan maintains its own claim, places the islands within Yilan County, and refers to them as the Diaoyutai Islands.

Three names. One tiny island group.
Japan administers the islands; China and Taiwan claim them. The name on a map can reveal which geographic framework the mapmaker is using.

If the islands themselves are unchanged, what does changing their name tell you about the map?

Explore why these tiny islands matter

They are uninhabited — but strategically placed

The islands themselves are tiny, but their location affects surrounding maritime space, fishing access, and control of nearby waters in the East China Sea.

The dispute is larger than the land

The competing names sit on top of a sovereignty dispute involving administration, national claims, and the maritime geography around the islands — not just the islands' few square miles of land.
