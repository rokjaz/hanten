H001 · Mean & median · ~5 minutes
ASK

What’s the average income at this BBQ?

Ten people are at a backyard BBQ. These are their annual salaries.

What would you say the “average” person here earns?
Example incomes are invented; the arithmetic is exact.
PREDICT

A guest earning $50 million a year walks in.

Nobody else’s salary changes.

+$50,000,000 guest

What happens to the mean?

Does it barely move, rise somewhat, or explode upward?

What happens to the median?

Does it behave the same way—or differently?

Commit to a prediction before the reveal.
REVEAL

One new person. Two very different “averages.”

Median
$80,500

$86,400
After the $50M guest arrives
Median

No one else got richer. Only the summary changed.
UNDERSTAND

Why did the two numbers react so differently?

Mean

The mean uses every value. One extreme salary can pull it dramatically upward.

Median

The median is the middle value. A single extreme value usually changes it much less.

Neither is automatically “better.” The useful measure depends on the question.
TRANSFER

Take the idea somewhere else.

A multimillionaire moves into a small town. The town’s average income rises sharply.
Did the typical resident become richer?
When someone tells you the “average,” what do you need to know before deciding what it means?
